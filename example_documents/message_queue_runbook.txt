MESSAGE QUEUE INCIDENT RUNBOOK

Purpose
This runbook describes how to diagnose and recover from message queue backlog and consumer failure incidents.

Symptoms
- Queue depth increases continuously while incoming traffic remains stable.
- Consumers stop acknowledging messages.
- Message age and processing delay rise above their normal thresholds.
- Downstream services receive events late or do not receive them at all.
- Dead-letter queue size may increase.

Probable Causes
- One or more consumers have crashed or become unhealthy.
- A poison message repeatedly causes the consumer to fail.
- Consumer processing is slower than the message production rate.
- The message broker cannot deliver messages because of a network or storage problem.
- Consumer authentication credentials have expired.

Immediate Response
1. Confirm the affected queue, current queue depth, oldest message age, and active consumer count.
2. Check consumer health logs and identify the first consumer that stopped acknowledging messages.
3. Inspect the dead-letter queue for poison messages before restarting any service.
4. Restart only the unhealthy consumer when a safe restart procedure is available.
5. Temporarily scale healthy consumers if the backlog continues to grow.
6. Verify that queue depth and message age decrease after recovery.

Prevention
- Alert when queue depth or oldest message age exceeds the established baseline.
- Add dead-letter handling and retry limits for poison messages.
- Monitor consumer acknowledgement rate and processing latency.
- Use idempotent message handlers so retries do not create duplicate business operations.
- Document safe consumer restart and backlog recovery procedures.

Escalation
Escalate to the messaging platform owner when the broker is unavailable, storage is exhausted, multiple queues are affected, or consumer recovery does not reduce the backlog.
